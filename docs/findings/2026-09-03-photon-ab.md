# Findings — the Photon A/B: two robust rows, one the method cannot call

**Date:** 2026-09-03
**Runs:** `693303490917119`, `325049271562727`, `236314776904974` —
three replicate pairs of `databricks_job.photon_ab`, all six arms SUCCESS.
Phase 1's calibration slice (2025-08-13, 24 hours) through Bronze →
Silver → Gold, timed per layer.
**Method:** each arm's own `ArmMeasurement` JSON, retrieved from
`/Volumes/almanac_dbx/burn/photon_ab/` after every run (the job overwrites
the same two paths, so they are pulled between runs).

The §8.2 hypothesis was **pre-registered in the design doc before any
measurement** and is reproduced here unedited:

> Photon helps silver and gold materially, helps bronze little or nothing
> (bronze is almost all JSON parsing, which Photon covers only partially),
> and the blend may be a wash or a loss.

---

## The result

All three replicates report `compressed_gb = 2.0124815106391907` on both
arms — byte-identical, so every pair measured the same slice.

| Layer | r1 | r2 | r3 | mean | range | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bronze | 1.18 | 1.05 | 1.23 | 1.15 | 1.05–1.23 | **indeterminate** |
| Silver | 1.96 | 2.40 | 2.06 | **2.14** | 1.96–2.40 | helped |
| Gold | 1.45 | 1.29 | 1.42 | **1.38** | 1.29–1.45 | helped |

```
conclusive: false   hypothesis_holds: null   paid_for_itself: null
```

**Silver ~2.1x and Gold ~1.38x are the defensible results.** Every
replicate clears the 1.10 materiality threshold with room to spare.

## Why Bronze cannot be called, and why that is the finding

The decision rule was **fixed before replicate 3 was run**: the whole
observed range must clear the threshold, or the effect is not separable
from noise. Bronze's range straddles it, so the verdict is withheld — even
though the mean of 1.15 is above 1.10 and two of three replicates cleared
it. Switching to the mean after seeing the data is exactly the move the
rule exists to prevent.

The reason is the noise floor. Re-running an **identical** arm:

| arm | layer | spread across replicates |
|---|---|---|
| standard | bronze | 2.3% |
| standard | silver | 15.0% |
| standard | gold | **29.9%** |
| photon | bronze | **19.9%** |
| photon | silver | 6.9% |
| photon | gold | 18.2% |

**The Bronze effect under test (~15%) is smaller than the run-to-run noise
of the arms measuring it.** Three samples cannot resolve that.

## The claim this document previously made, and why it was wrong

An earlier version of this file, written from run `693303490917119`
alone, stated that **"the hypothesis is falsified"** because that run
computed Bronze at 1.18x — "helped", contradicting the prediction.

That was over-claimed. Run `325049271562727` computed Bronze at 1.05x —
"no material change" — and therefore that the hypothesis **holds**. Two
single-run A/Bs, opposite verdicts, same code, same slice.

The honest statement is neither: **the measurement never had the power to
support either verdict.** "Refuted" and "cannot tell" are different
claims, and the first is much stronger. Recorded here rather than quietly
edited, since §13 requires the same of the hypothesis itself.

## The harness was reporting a verdict it could not support

The defect was in this project's own code, and it is the
threshold-vs-distribution mistake:

- `hypothesis_holds` returned a **`bool` from a single run**, comparing a
  noisy measurement against a hard 1.10 threshold with no notion of
  confidence.
- `paid_for_itself` returned `true` from `dbus_consumed = 0.0`, because
  with DBUs at zero the cost model collapses to VM time — and Photon's
  *entire* cost penalty is its DBU premium (the $/DBU rate is identical
  for Photon and non-Photon; `2026-09-01-azure-pricing.md`). So it
  systematically favoured Photon.

Both now refuse the question: `compare_arms` takes replicates, verdicts
are range-based and may be `indeterminate`, `hypothesis_holds` is
`bool | None`, and `paid_for_itself` is `None` until DBU totals exist.
`_run_cost` averages, so replicate count cannot inflate cost.

## The decision: do not enable Photon

> **Superseded in part, 2026-09-03 — and both errors ran the same way.**
> The DBU totals were obtained the same day
> (`2026-09-03-measured-dbus.md`), so the cost verdict below is no longer
> withheld: **`paid_for_itself: False`**, `cost_off $0.40` against
> `cost_on $0.74`. Photon costs **1.85x** for a 1.31x speedup.
>
> The break-even table below is also **arithmetically wrong**: it used
> `V = $0.474/node-hour` as the VM rate, but that figure is VM (`$0.249`)
> **plus the assumed DBU cost** — double-counting DBUs into `V` while also
> charging them through `d`. Corrected against the measured DBU rate, break-
> even is **1.31 / 1.54 / 1.65**, not 1.55 / 1.96 / 2.16. The measured
> multiplier is **2.297** (range 2.118–2.394), which clears every corrected
> threshold and every uncorrected one too.
>
> The recommendation is unchanged; its strength is not. This was "a wash on
> assumed inputs" and is now **a measured loss with no plausible input that
> rescues it**. Both mistakes — the inflated `V` and the assumed ~2x
> multiplier — happened to flatter Photon, which is why the conclusion
> survived them. The original reasoning is kept below rather than rewritten.

The cost question cannot be answered directly (no DBU totals until billing
settles), so it was inverted: **how large can Photon's DBU-per-hour
multiplier be before it stops paying for itself?**

Applied to the *backfill's* measured shape — fetch 1,292.9 s, Bronze
11,282.5 s, Silver 4,994.5 s — with Silver at 2.14x and Bronze varied
across its measured range:

| Bronze speedup | Overall speedup | Break-even DBU multiplier |
|---|---|---|
| 1.00 (no help) | 1.18x | **1.55** |
| 1.15 (mean) | 1.31x | **1.96** |
| 1.23 (best case) | 1.37x | **2.16** |

Photon's DBU multiplier is commonly ~2x. **Break-even straddles it**, and
it keeps straddling across a plausible 0.5–1.0 DBU/node-hour range for the
assumed input (1.79–2.28 at the mean), so the conclusion does not rest on
that assumption.

**Photon is a wash on this workload, and the leg that would decide it is
the leg that cannot be measured.** That is a genuine result, and it
matches §8.2's own allowance that "the blend may be a wash or a loss".

**The real lever is elsewhere.** Bronze is **63.8%** of execution and is
the layer Photon helps least — because it is single-threaded on
non-splittable gzip (`2026-09-02-bronze-is-single-threaded.md`), running
one task on one core while the cluster idles. Fixing that is worth far
more than 1.15x, and it would also remove the ambiguity from this very
question by shrinking the layer that dominates the blend.

## What is still open

- **DBU totals** for the six task runs, from the billing system tables,
  which would turn `paid_for_itself` from `None` into a real answer. The
  break-even table above is what to check them against.
- **The variance itself is uncontrolled.** The arms run concurrently with
  no `depends_on`, and cluster provisioning differed by 441 s vs 111 s in
  one run, so they contend unequally. More replicates of a confounded
  design buy precision on a biased estimate; controlling the overlap is
  the fix, not a larger n.
